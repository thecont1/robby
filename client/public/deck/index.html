A two-sided image

What if an image had a reverse?

Digital photographs normally have one visible face. Their origins, transformations, credentials, colour material, and computational history are usually hidden, fragmented, stripped away, or inaccessible.

Troid proposes a different image form — a digital photograph can become a two-sided object, like a postcard or a coin. One face is the photograph. The other is a related, deterministic, inspectable image.

A photograph can have another side

An Obverse–Reverse Image Object

Meet the orio cookie

Obverse

The original photograph

Supplied by the user. It remains sacred and byte-for-byte untouched — the source artifact is never edited by the compiler.

Reverse

A deterministic image

Generated by the compiler from declared rules, colour material, identity, and bounded evidence. The reverse belongs to this obverse and no other.

Recipe

The author's program

A compact, user-authored program that determines how the reverse is made — lexed, parsed, validated, and lowered to canonical IR before anything renders.

The two faces are mutually exclusive to sight — you turn between them. The Trace (the visible account of what the compiler did) accompanies every orio.

You turn between the two faces

The format and the compiler

troid and robby

Troid is the Reverse-Obverse Image Duality: the format, the concept, and the two-sided image-object itself. Robby is the explainable compiler and working museum that produces and displays it.

The gallery gives the compiler corporeality — before compilation it is a calm photo viewer; after an intentional request, it becomes a laboratory.

The duality · the compiler

Computation is not ambient decoration

A conscious act of computation

Robby does not silently process every photograph in the gallery. Five intentional steps run one after another — select the image, read the recipe, compile an orio, watch the counter, and turn to the reverse when it resolves.

This matters because compute is not ambient decoration. It is a deliberate authorial act — the compile happens because a person chose to make it happen.

Compile is a deliberate act

Authored, lexed, validated

The recipe is the author's instrument

The user writes a compact, human-readable program. It is not a fake settings panel — the compiler submits the authored text verbatim.

A recipe edit makes an earlier reverse visibly stale until the user compiles again. Invalid drafts are not silently fixed or guessed by the UI.

Verbatim, never guessed

Eight stages, visible

The Teppanyaki Counter

The Teppanyaki Counter is Robby's live observability surface. It rests as a quiet, transparent hairline enclosure beside the photograph.

When the user requests an orio, it comes alive — not with simulated progress, but with the compiler's actual staged work. Explainability as performance.

Live, visible, deterministic

What becomes legible, when

Eight visible stages of an orio

Act I · Read the source

Intake · Read · Measure

The immutable source artifact and its byte identity; available EXIF/IPTC/XMP evidence and C2PA credential status; canonical pixel identity and image-derived measurements.

Act II · Author the program

Split · Declare

Deterministic palette extraction and weighted colour material; the authored recipe and its canonical compiler IR — submitted verbatim.

Act III · Produce the artifact

Bind · Resolve · Marry

The reproducibility relationship among source, pixels, recipe, policy, evidence, and runtime; deterministic rendering of the reverse; and the binding of obverse and reverse into the session's orio cookie.

Each stage makes a different thing legible — source bytes, evidence, measurements, palette, author intent, reproducibility, the rendered artifact, and the binding.

INTAKE → READ → MEASURE → SPLIT → DECLARE → BIND → RESOLVE → MARRY

A real compiler stack

A real compiler, not a filter

Robby source flows through a Rust lexer, parser, validator, and canonical IR — then through measurement, palette split, and binding, before a deterministic renderer produces the reverse image and runtime record.

The IR wire format stays intentionally stable as robby-ir-v1 while the compiler and execution contracts evolve behind it.

rust → canonical IR → renderer

Effective K, clamped

Palette truth

Robby distinguishes between what the author asks for and what the source can yield.

When an image has fewer distinct colours than the requested palette size, Robby should clamp honestly and explain why — rather than fabricate swatches, duplicate colours, or fail mysteriously.

Clamp, then explain why

A deterministic object

The binding is not ownership

Robby forms a deterministic object binding from real compile inputs: source bytes, canonical pixels, the recipe, the canonical IR, the disclosure policy, the approved evidence, and the runtime versions.

The binding answers one question — does this reverse belong to this exact source, recipe, policy, and runtime? It does not claim copyright, authorship, ownership, or metaphysical truth.

Binding, not authorship

Five run-bound states

C2PA — precise, not performative

Status states

5 run-bound outcomes

NOT INSPECTED · ABSENT · PRESENT (valid, trusted signer) · PRESENT (valid, untrusted signer) · PRESENT (invalid) · UNAVAILABLE. The result is shared across counter, provenance, manifest, and orio.

Critical rule

The reverse never inherits

A Robby reverse never inherits or impersonates the obverse's C2PA credential. C2PA is inspected from the original, untouched source bytes — never from generated output.

NOT INSPECTED · ABSENT · PRESENT · VALID · TRUSTED · UNTRUSTED · INVALID · UNAVAILABLE

Run-bound, never inherited

Never edit the source

Sacred obverses

Robby must never

Five prohibitions

Rewrite the source file. Recompress or resize it in place. Strip or alter EXIF, IPTC, XMP, ICC, or C2PA data. Overwrite the source with a derivative. Write generated reverse files into a watched source folder.

A viewer that becomes a laboratory

Categories, separated

Explainability is more than a log

Observed & Verified

Source facts

An EXIF field that exists in the supplied file (Observed); a C2PA validation result (Verified). Both are properties of the source bytes — not of the compile.

Measured & Derived

Compile outputs

A canonical pixel hash or palette weight (Measured); a binding digest or seeded arrangement (Derived). These come from the compile and are reproducible from declared inputs.

Declared & Redacted

Author choices

The user's recipe and disclosure policy (Declared); GPS, timestamp, filename, raw metadata (Redacted). The system shows what it knows, how it knows it, and what it does not.

Robby separates categories that are often conflated in a single "explainability" log. This is the project's epistemic discipline.

What it knows · how · and what it doesn't

Not whether, but what kind

What remains alive

Robby is advanced, but intentionally unfinished in the most important artistic sense. The open question is not whether it can produce a reverse — it can.

The question is what kind of reverse becomes a work of art worth preserving and sharing. The current observability sheet is the studio table — the final visual language will emerge through iteration with those materials.
